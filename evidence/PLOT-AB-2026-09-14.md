# `plot` and the 220 s ceiling — one session, two tags

**14 September 2026, 16:15–16:56 UTC, jl-i (taipan-main-01).**

## The question this was built to settle

Across three release measurements the `plot` mission hit `mission_eval.py`'s
`DEFAULT_TURN_WAIT = 220.0` ceiling **v1.2.0 0/3, v1.3.1 1/3, v1.4.0 3/3**, and
the release-ablation report read that as v1.4.0 regressing. The judais-lobi
cross-arm analysis (evidence `5e7209b`) disagreed: the requests were
byte-identical across the arms bar our own +143-character `compute_nodes` tool
description, the decode rate was constant at ~42 tok/s, and the heavy tail —
thousands of completion tokens in **one** call — was already present at v1.1.3
and varied **per serving session**, not per tag.

Those two readings cannot both be right, and no measurement so far could tell
them apart, because every arm had been measured against **its own** model
session. So: hold the serving session constant and vary only the tag.

**The answer: the tail is on BOTH tags.** In one serving session, on the same
two cards, against the same vLLM process, `plot` hit the ceiling **1 of 5 times
on v1.2.0 — the champion — and 2 of 5 times on v1.4.0.** The release-ablation
row's 0/3-vs-3/3 is not a property of the release.

## The design actually used

| block | tag | pane | model | plots |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | v1.2.0 | :8771, MANAGED (`--model-script`) | leased 2 L4s, **served session 1** | 5 |
| 2 | v1.4.0 | :8772, **UNMANAGED** (no `--model-script`, `--model-endpoint` at session 1) | **the same vLLM process**, pid unchanged | 5 |
| 3 | v1.2.0 | :8771, MANAGED, after a full release | **fresh** session 2, same two cards | 3 |

**Which of the two shapes the brief offered:** the unmanaged one. Block 2's
pane was started with **no `--model-script`** and `--model-endpoint` pointed at
session 1's published URL, so it could find a model and could not start one —
it adopted the endpoint and took no lease. Block 1's pane was never stopped; it
held the lease throughout. **Proof the process did not change:** the vLLM pid
was read immediately before and after every single eval and recorded in each
run's `.meta`; it is `2949014` on all ten rows of blocks 1 and 2, and
`3714782` on all three rows of block 3.

**Keeping session 1 alive through block 2 needed one thing.**
`mission/model.py` releases the cards after `--model-idle-minutes 15` of
nobody using the model *and* nobody active on a page, and block 2 is ~20
minutes of quiet on :8771. A page's own heartbeat is the designed answer:
`POST /api/model {"intent":"interest"}` — which `mission/service.py` documents
as *"spends nothing and leases nothing"* and which takes no credential for the
lease itself — every 20 s (`heartbeat.sh`). `/api/model` reported
`interested: 1` throughout. The shared-use marks that would otherwise have
carried the fact live in each pane's **own** state directory, so the v1.4.0
pane's use was invisible to the v1.2.0 pane's reaper; the heartbeat is what
closed that gap.

## What the killed call actually was

**Every first call carries `prompt_tokens = 87038`, identically, on both tags.**
That is the judais analysis's byte-identical request, confirmed here. The whole
outcome turns on how long that one call keeps generating:

* v1.2.0, session 1: 2655, 1846, 1381, 2511, **≈9206 and still going**
* v1.2.0, session 2: 3227, 1813, 1955
* v1.4.0, session 1: **6490**, 617, 1529, **5169**, **5860**

A-run5 is the clean case, because block 1 had one pane on the server: judais
recorded **zero** calls and the run's `meta.json` carries `orphaned_at`, while
the served counter climbed at a **dead-constant 41.6–42.4 tok/s for the entire
220 s**. One call. ~9.2k tokens. It never returned.

**And the 220 s ceiling does not stop it.** `vllm:request_success_total{abort}`
is **0 on every run in the study**: `mission_eval` stops polling, it does not
cancel, so the request runs on. A-run5's call was still emitting at ~42 tok/s
**three minutes after** the harness had given up — another ~6.9k tokens,
`num_requests_running` still 1 — and only stopped when the pane was torn down.
That is a runaway generation that never terminates, not a turn that is merely
slow, and it is the same shape on both tags.

## The fresh-server block

Session 2 was a genuinely new server: session 1 fully released (cards to
0 MiB, vLLM gone, broker `0 running / 0 queued / 0 sessions`) before the pane
was restarted, giving vLLM pid `3714782` on a new port. v1.2.0 then went
**3/3 clean, no ceiling**, with first-call completions of 3227 / 1813 / 1955 —
the same middle of the distribution session 1 produced. **Three draws is three
draws:** this block shows the fresh session behaving like the old one, and it
does **not** have the power to show that sessions never differ. What it does
rule out is the reading that session 2 was somehow the "bad" condition.

## The serving-side log, and why it is `/metrics` instead

The coordinator's addition was right that a killed call's tokens are only
recoverable from the serving side, and the vLLM's own log is **not reachable on
this principal**: the server runs as `tj-jgompert-53ae078c` with a private home
(`/home/tj-jgompert-53ae078c/jobs/...`, permission denied) and owner-only
`/proc/<pid>/fd`, and the broker's `GET /v1/jobs/<id>/logs` returns the session
wrapper's own lines plus systemd's teardown, never the engine's. It is also
launched `--disable-log-requests`, so per-request lines would not exist even if
the log were readable.

`http://127.0.0.1:<port>/metrics` is reachable, unauthenticated on loopback,
and exact. `metrics_sampler.sh` polled it every 5 s and is the record filed
beside each launch line. It is better than a throughput line would have been:
`generation_tokens_total` is a counter, so the delta across a turn IS that
turn's generated tokens rather than an integration, and
`request_success_total{finished_reason}` is what proved nothing is ever
aborted.

## What this says about the ledger

The `2026-09-14 release v1.2.0 vs v1.4.0` row's **completion** regression was
called "the sturdier finding, because the `plot` timeout reproduced 3 times out
of 3 with an identical signature". That reasoning does not survive this
measurement: the signature reproduces on the champion too, once the champion is
given five draws instead of three in a session where a tail occurs. The
`plot` scenario is a **coin flip on a heavy-tailed first-call length**, and
three runs cannot distinguish two tags that both flip it.

Nothing here rehabilitates v1.4.0 on accuracy — that is a different criterion,
measured on different missions, and this study did not touch it. What it
removes is `plot` as evidence for the completion column.

## Two defects in the runner, both fixed, one of them caught the hard way

1. **`local a="$1" b="${a}x"` is one command**, so every word is expanded
   before any of them is assigned; under `set -u` the second reads an unbound
   `a`. It killed the first attempt 55 s in, before anything was leased.
2. **A `\`-continued line followed by an empty line ends the command.** The
   pane invocation emitted the optional `--model-script` flag as *its own
   line*, so the unmanaged arm's command split in two: the first half ran
   `python -m taipan.mission` with **no `--bind`/`--port`**, collided with a
   default already in use, and died; the second half was read as a command
   named `--model-endpoint`. The pid the runner then held was not a pane.
   Because the runner's `trap cleanup EXIT` would have released a serving
   session that four evals had already been measured against, the runner was
   stopped with **SIGKILL specifically so its trap would not run**, and blocks
   2 and 3 were driven by hand (`pane_b.sh`, `pane_a.sh`, `runblock.sh`) with
   the same guards. Session 1 survived intact; the vLLM pid is the proof.

Both are fixed in `plot_ab.sh`, and the flag is now emitted inside the endpoint
line's own slot so it can never be a blank line.

## Unverified, and worth saying

* **Block 2's served-token counts are upper bounds** (see †). Its judais
  per-call numbers are exact; its `served gen` column is not per-turn.
* **A-run1 has no served figure**: the metrics sampler was started at 16:19:55,
  after that run. Its judais record is complete.
* **Five draws per tag in one session, three in the second.** Enough to show
  the tail is not tag-specific — it occurred on both — and *not* enough to put
  a rate on it for either tag. Nobody should read 1/5 vs 2/5 as v1.4.0 being
  twice as likely to tail.
* **The `--cognition` arm was not touched**, as in every prior run.
* **Not diagnosed: why the first call fails to terminate.** This says what it
  is — one call, `prompt_tokens` 87038, generating at the constant decode rate
  until something tears the server down, on both tags — and does not say why
  the model does not emit a stop.
* **A second concurrent stream existed during parts of block 2** and its
  source was not isolated beyond "the un-cancelled previous turn and the
  v1.4.0 pane's own startup work". It does not affect the per-stream decode
  rate, which held at ~41 tok/s throughout.

## The evidence scan

154 files, 3,679 candidate strings of 24 characters or more, hashed and looked
up against 93 sha256 digests of every credential value on jl-i — computed **on
jl-i**, so no value crossed the VPN in either direction and none was printed.
**Zero matches. Zero JWT-shaped strings** (`eyJ…`.`…`.`…`), zero
`Bearer <token>` occurrences.

Two things the first pass turned up, both resolved:

* **21 `eyJ`-shaped substrings, all incidental.** Every one sits inside a
  `data:image/png;base64,…` — the matplotlib chart the `plot` mission is
  supposed to produce. `eyJ` is what `{"` encodes to, so it turns up inside any
  long base64 blob by chance; none is a token, and the JWT shape proper (three
  dot-separated segments) appears zero times.
* **One credential "match" that was a variable NAME.** `taipan.toml` has
  `client_secret_env = "TAIPAN_COGNITO_CLIENT_SECRET"` — a pointer saying where
  the secret lives. At 28 characters it cleared `token-hashes.py`'s length
  floor and was hashed as though it were a secret, and it then matched that
  same name inside the hash file's own `env:cognito.env:<KEY>` labels. The
  reported "pool credential in the evidence" was the hash table finding itself.
  `token-hashes.py` now skips `*_env`/`*_file`/`*_path`-style pointer keys, and
  the hash table is kept **outside** the tree it scans — a scanning aid is not
  evidence. Worth keeping: a scanner that cries wolf on a variable name is a
  scanner people start ignoring.
